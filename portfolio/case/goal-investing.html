the context

financial planning is quietly broken for most people. the top 1% have a dedicated wealth manager. everyone else is left to work it out across unstructured savings, high-interest emis and stock market jargon that seems designed to keep them out.

the platform was conceived to close that gap: automated, unbiased financial planning, built on low-cost, multi-asset etfs rather than on a salesperson's commission.

the problem

first-time investors do not think in alpha, expense ratios or market cap. they think in life events: buying a car, paying for a wedding, building a cushion for the month something goes wrong.

so the job for version one was translation. take the mechanical complexity of etf investing and package it into a journey built on goals, one that feels achievable rather than intimidating.

nobody has an investment problem. people have a wedding in two years.

the architecture under the screens

the foundation was structural before it was visual. these are long-term decisions people are making with their own money, so the app was built on constraints that could carry them, not on screens that happened to scale.

  1. a goal becomes a portfolioa subjective input, like i need ₹5 lakh for a trip in three years, translates systematically into an optimised, risk-adjusted etf portfolio. the whole product depends on that mapping holding.
  2. data collection as a conversationregulatory and risk data is notoriously high friction to collect. i broke the information architecture down so each step asks for less at once, while still capturing data precise enough for the recommendation engine behind it.
  3. a system built for what comes nextmarket shifts, portfolio rebalancing and new goal types were all coming. the design system had to absorb them without breaking the primary hierarchy of the screens.

onboarding: habits before products

people are not introduced to the platform by choosing stocks. they start by describing their current financial habits.

the mandatory parts, risk assessment and financial profiling, are where people usually leave. so they are broken into small, approachable steps, each one easy to answer, rather than one long form that asks for everything at the start.

the dashboard: an anchor, not a ticker

the dashboard is the financial anchor, the screen people come back to. so it does not flash red and green market tickers, which mostly produce anxiety. it leads with overall portfolio health and progress toward each goal.

tracking stays free of jargon, and the flat-fee structure is broken down in plain view. trust was the thing being designed here: the comparison people carry in their heads is a commission-based advisor, and a visible, flat fee is the answer to it.

goals, short and long

the ui adapts to the timeline of the goal, because the right investment for a holiday next year is not the right one for a house in ten.

  • short-term goals a near target, like a holiday in eighteen months, is guided toward lower-risk, highly liquid etf allocations.
  • long-term goals a distant milestone, like a house downpayment in ten years, is guided toward growth-oriented etfs, so compounding stops being an abstract idea and becomes something people can act on today.

explaining without the jargon

alongside the core journey sit the informative screens, the ones that explain what is happening to someone's money without assuming they already know the vocabulary.

how it landed

version one went from concept to high-fidelity ui in four months, researched, mapped and designed from the ground up. it set down the platform's core promise, that expert financial advice should be available to everyone, and kept the person focused on their own milestones rather than on the market.

version one shipped. the product has moved through many versions since and looks nothing like this today, so what you are seeing is the app as it launched, not as it is now. there are no usage numbers on this page.

what version one set down was the structure: goals that translate into portfolios, data collection that does not scare people off, and a system with room for rebalancing and new goal types.

consumer · shipped, not measured

make investing feel achievable.

for people who have never invested and are put off by the vocabulary. the stated product is automated planning on low-cost etfs. the real product is getting a nervous first-timer through a regulatory risk questionnaire without losing them.

goals first, products second.

onboarding that starts from habits, not stocks. a dashboard that shows progress toward goals instead of market movement. goal creation that changes its advice with the timeline. and underneath it, a mapping from a life event to a risk-adjusted portfolio.

the same move, on a different kind of user.

nobody has an investment problem. people have a wedding in two years. that is the move that runs through his b2b work, taking the brief one level down, applied to a consumer for once.

and the fee decision is the quiet one worth noticing: showing a flat fee in plain view is a trust argument made against a commission advisor the user is picturing, not against a competitor on a feature list.

he can design for the public.

the strongest objection to this portfolio is that it is all b2b tools. this answers part of it: a consumer mobile product, a regulated domain, and a user who is anxious rather than busy.

it answers the design half. it does not answer the outcome half, because there is no outcome on the page.

the honest caveats.

there are no usage, conversion or retention numbers. version one shipped and the product has been rebuilt many times since, so these screens are history rather than the current app. he says so rather than presenting them as live.

the goal-to-portfolio mapping and the recommendation engine are described, not shown. the architecture claim is that the design was built on constraints that could carry it. that is plausible and it is not demonstrated here.

ask what happened to onboarding drop-off once real people went through the risk questions, and how much of this structure survived the rebuilds.
