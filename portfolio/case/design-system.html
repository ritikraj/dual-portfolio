the problem under the problem

the stated problem was speed. design was the queue everything waited in, and a system is the standard answer to that.

the actual problem was that the company was mid-identity-change. it had sold itself as a marketing tool and was becoming an ai-first real estate operating system. a system built for the first one would need tearing out within a year of the second one arriving. deciding which company i was building for was the real first decision, and it was not a design decision.

the bet

every component was engineered to hold ai before engineering wrote a line of ai. ai variants on cards, loaders and graphs. headers tested with the ai elements present and absent, so neither state was the special case.

that looks like over-engineering right up until the roadmap turns, and then it looks like the only sane thing anyone did that quarter. it is a sequencing bet, and sequencing is most of what a system is.

a strong design system is 80% thinking and 20% figma.

the architecture

token architecture in figma variables, carrying multi-theme and multi-context from the start rather than retrofitting either. 120+ coded components standing against 1,000+ figma components. accessibility audited to wcag 2.1 aa rather than asserted.

i benchmarked against atlassian, polaris, lightning and spectrum. not to copy them. to know exactly which of their decisions i was declining, and why.

how it landed

<1%detach rate
120+coded components
1,000+figma components
~30%faster to delivery

the detach rate is the number i actually watch. adoption can be mandated. a detach rate under one percent means designers stopped needing to fight the system to do their job, and that is the only honest measure of whether a system is real.

the tenth one

i have built ten or more design systems. most of them failed, and they failed the same way: built as a component library, handed over as a component library, quietly abandoned when the product changed shape.

this one worked because the thinking went in before the file did, and because governance was treated as part of the design rather than as admin that happens afterwards.

you have read my account. there is another one.

tokens, variables, 120+ coded components, aa audited.

my read, not his numbers. user impact is the lowest here and that is not a criticism: a design system reaches users through other people’s work. organisational impact is close to the ceiling, and the detach rate under one percent is the single most credible number on his site.

he watches the right number.

adoption can be mandated. a detach rate under one percent cannot. he picked the metric that could embarrass him, which tells you he was trying to learn something rather than trying to report something.

and he is straight about the other nine attempts that failed. ten design systems, one that stuck, and he leads with the ratio rather than burying it.

the honest caveats.

~30% faster design-to-delivery is the softest figure in his portfolio. delivery speed has a dozen inputs and a design system is one of them. i would treat it as directionally true and not as an attribution.

the detach rate, the component counts and the aa audit are all checkable inside the company. those are the ones to ask about.
